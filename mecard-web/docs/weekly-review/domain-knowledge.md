# Smart business card industry: running notes

Kept by the scheduled weekly UX/growth review. Each run adds to it; newest notes go at the bottom of each section with a date.
Background notes come from general industry knowledge and are not yet checked against live sources unless a source is given.

## Market shape
- (2026-11-16) Global players: Popl, Blinq, HiHello, Linq, Mobilo, V1CE, Dot, Wave. Most have moved from selling one-off NFC cards to per-seat team subscriptions, with the card treated as the acquisition hook.
- (2026-11-16) Buyers split into solo professionals (price-sensitive, buy once) and teams (HR, marketing or sales leaders who buy for 10–500 staff and care about brand control, admin and leavers). MeCard is right to lead with teams.

## What team buyers expect
- (2026-11-16) Central brand control (logo, colours, locked fields), bulk add from CSV or directory sync, deactivate or reassign when staff leave.
- (2026-11-16) Lead capture: a two-way contact exchange form on the profile, with leads pushed to a CRM (HubSpot, Salesforce) or emailed. This is the most common "Pro" feature competitors sell on.
- (2026-11-16) Other sharing channels beside the card: Apple/Google Wallet pass, QR on email signature, virtual video-call backgrounds, phone NFC tag.
- (2026-11-16) Proof of ROI: admin dashboards showing taps, saves and leads per person.

## Pricing patterns
- (2026-11-16) Competitors mostly charge per seat per month or year. MeCard's once-off Pro is a clear point of difference for South African buyers wary of USD subscriptions; worth saying plainly against "no monthly fees".

## South African context
- (2026-11-16) EFT is still a common way for businesses to pay; quotes and invoices with a PO step matter for corporate buyers. Fast local delivery to the metros is a selling point against imported cards.
